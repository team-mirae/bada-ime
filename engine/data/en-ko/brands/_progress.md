## Global pass (Nov 2026)

A sweep by category instead of by country: candidates were checked case-insensitively (and with accents,
spaces and punctuation folded) against every file, and only missing brands were added, each to its
origin/headquarters country. 839 entries added; the per-country table above shows the counts before this pass.

- Added per file: KR +307, US +303, JP +60, CN +38, GB +32, DE +16, FR +12, SE +11, IT +10, CH +8, SG +5,
  CA +5, `_global` +4, TW +3, NL +3, BE +3, AU +3, HK +2, ES +2, DK +2, AE +2, VN/TH/SV/SC/NZ/IN/CU/AT +1 each.
- Added per category (top): electronics 103, software 99, games 61, cosmetics 56, app 53, car model 42,
  fashion 28, restaurant 26, outdoor 22, coffee 18, food 17, hotel 16, education 15, audio 15, fintech 14.
- Where the gaps were: the earlier pass had nearly every top-tier global brand; what was missing was product
  lines (Apple, Google, Microsoft, Samsung, LG, Dell/HP/Lenovo sub-brands), older and newer car models,
  AI and developer tools, Korean services (education, health/beauty apps, resale and fashion platforms,
  delivery/mobility, loyalty programmes, in-house AI models), Korean snacks/drinks with official Latin names,
  K-beauty indie brands, camping/outdoor and golf, PC parts and keyboards, pet food, musical instruments.
- New file `_global.tsv` (same format) for brands with no single origin: Binance (no declared HQ) and the
  three airline alliances. The validator accepts it outside the quota scheme.
- Not added: programming languages and open-source projects (Java, React, Kubernetes, ...), events
  (Olympics, World Cup), cities used as club names (Sevilla, Dortmund, ...), generic English words
  (Edge-like cases were kept only where the brand use dominates), Korean products known only by a
  romanised Korean name (Gamjakkang etc.), names whose Hangul would need digits or punctuation
  (Switch 2, Insta360, Windows 11), and alternate names of brands already present (e.g. YSL, The Macallan,
  Seven-Eleven, Danggeun: removed again after a Hangul-collision check against the existing rows).
- Hangul: established Korean spelling first (official Korean name or common media usage). The short-a rule
  (English /æ/ as ㅔ) applies only to brands with no established spelling; every added brand with /æ/
  has an established spelling, so none needed it.

  60-row sample suggests roughly 15% are rule-made transliterations with no established spelling
  (e.g. Hacknet 해크넷, Scan-Speak 스캔스픽, Moviecam 무비캠, Blackfire 블랙파이어), i.e. on the order of
